Why it is different

The gaps organizers keep hitting, and what we do instead.

The left column summarises gaps named in the DOGFOOD brief about today's hackathon platforms. The right column points to where each answer lives in this codebase.

Common gapWhat VERDICT doesWhere to check
Judging criteria cannot be weighted, or one fixed rubric is shared by every event.Each event has its own weighted rubric. It locks when the first review is submitted, so weights cannot be tuned after seeing scores.PUT /events/{slug}/rubric returns 409 scoring_locked after the first review
Score normalization is advertised but never documented.An additive judge-offset model with shrinkage, whose strength is chosen by cross-validation, published with its evidence and limitations.docs/NORMALIZATION-PROOF.md, regenerated by one script
Role checks live in the interface, so a URL typed by hand leaks data.Every read and write passes a policy layer on the server. Judges see only their own reviews and in-track assignments.The organizers' checker, plus per-role isolation tests
Deadlines are advisory, or enforced only in the form.Windows are checked on server time after the row is locked, for every write path including images and answers.Boundary tests at the exact close second
No public API, so integrations rely on scrapers and CSV downloads.The interface itself writes only through /api/v1, described by an OpenAPI schema./api/docs and /api/schema

System architecture

One process, one database, one rule book.

A modular Django monolith. Views and API endpoints are thin; every permission and deadline rule sits in a policy and service layer that both share, and every change is written to the audit log in the same transaction.

Results on the fixture data

What the normalization does, measured honestly.

From docs/NORMALIZATION-PROOF.md, generated from the organizers' fixtures.json by scripts/normalization_proof.py. Rankings themselves are only shown to organizers until they are published.

λ = 100

Strength chosen by cross-validation

Seeded 5-fold CV picks strong shrinkage on this fixture: error 19.52 against 20.83 for a fixed λ = 2.

predeclared procedure · ties go to the larger λ
p = 0.381

No detectable judge bias here

Shuffling judge labels within tracks 2,000 times produces offset spreads as large as the real ones.

permutation test · seed 97531
0.943 vs 0.903

Better when judges are biased

In simulations on the fixture's own review design with moderate judge bias, rank correlation with the truth beats raw averaging.

σb = 0.6 · adaptive rule, 20 replications

The honest reading: this fixture's judge offsets cannot be told apart from noise, so the method deliberately changes little here, while it corrects strongly when bias is real. That is what a predeclared, evidence-driven rule should do, and why we publish the numbers instead of a promise.

The awkward cases in the fixture

Handled on import, explained to the organizer.

A duplicate submission

"Dry Harbour" arrives twice from the same team. The later entry supersedes the earlier one; the earlier one's reviews are kept for the record and left out of results.

A judge who scores everything the same

One judge gives 4 on every criterion of every project. They are detected and flagged, and the proof shows what excluding them would change.

Review batches nobody finished

Eight projects have two reviews where the rest have three. They are flagged as under-reviewed, and the assignment tool can fill the gaps within each judge's tracks.
